FLOOD FINDER V2 / USER GUIDE

From an address
to shared awareness.

Report local conditions, read independent weather data, and coordinate a response.

01 / EXPLORE

Start with your area

Open Incident map. No account is required to browse reports or weather. Enter a full U.S. street address in Weather location and choose Find weather, or pan the map and select Use current map area. This changes your view; it does not submit a report.

02 / FILTER

Find the reports you need

Select Flooding, Roads, Power, Medical / safety, Debris, Shelter, or Other. You can select multiple quick-type buttons; selected types are blue. All reports removes that restriction. The Report type dropdown chooses a single type. Combine these with status, priority, text search, or Only my reports after signing in. Fit reports frames the matching markers; the reset arrow clears filters.

03 / WEATHER

Know what is measured

Temperature, wind, humidity, and recent rain come from a nearby NWS station. Upcoming periods are NWS forecasts. Check the station observation time as well as fetch time. Not reported is missing data, not zero. Readings older than two hours are flagged. Nearby conditions can differ from the incident location; weather does not establish that a road is safe.

The official alert feed defaults to Florida and is configured by the server. Changing the weather address does not change that statewide alert feed. Turn on NWS alerts to see available alert polygons.

04 / ACCOUNT

Sign in to contribute

Create an account with your name, email, and a password of at least 12 characters. New accounts start as residents. If you select New report before signing in, the report form opens after login. Accounts are specific to the running app's database; they are not automatically copied between local versions.

05 / REPORT

Type an address. Publish.

Choose New report, pick the type and severity, and write a factual title and description. Enter the full street address, including number, city, state, and ZIP. Publish community report looks up the coordinates automatically. No map click is required.

Find address lets you check first. If several matches appear, choose the correct address before publishing. The U.S. Census service needs a street address; a business name alone may not match. Address searches are sent to Census, without your account or report description.

06 / LOCATION

Adjust only when needed

For an addressless incident or a location correction, expand Optional: adjust on map or enter coordinates. Select a pin, drag it, type coordinates, or use your device location with permission. Changing the typed address clears the old coordinates. Confirm that address text and the location refer to the same place.

Published report content, coordinates, address, and operational history are public. Avoid private names, phone numbers, or sensitive medical details. New reports stay unverified until reviewed.

07 / COMMAND CENTER

Review and dispatch

An administrator promotes registered crew leads to Responder, creates a response team, sets its availability and base coordinates, and selects a lead. Open an incident, review its status and priority, assign a team, and select Save and dispatch. Operational notes appear in the public incident history.

Rejected incidents cancel active assignments. Reopening with the same team creates a fresh assignment. A project owner must promote the first registered administrator using the server's admin command; registration alone never grants this access.

08 / MY ASSIGNMENTS

Track the response

Assigned team leads open My assignments and advance through Acknowledged, En route, On scene, and Completed. Progress moves one step at a time. Completed work resolves the incident. Cancelled or reassigned tasks cannot advance.

Report details also offer Show on map, Copy coordinates, and external navigation. Displayed routes are estimates, not verified flood-safe paths.

09 / CONNECTION

Understand update status

Live updates connected means incident changes can arrive automatically. Reconnecting means the live connection was interrupted. Use Refresh to retry. A report-refresh notice means visible information may be out of date. Weather is cached for about ten minutes; Refresh does not bypass the server cache.

10 / TROUBLESHOOT

When something is missing

If address lookup fails, add the street number, city, state, and ZIP, or use the optional map. A provider outage displays a different error from no match. If weather is unavailable, try a supported U.S. land location or check weather.gov. No weather is invented as a fallback. If the map cannot load, text reports and weather may still work. Clear filters if an expected report is hidden.

Experimental coordination app

Flood Finder is not an emergency service or a flood prediction system. For immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Never drive through floodwater or rely on community reports, station weather, or estimated routes as proof of safety.
