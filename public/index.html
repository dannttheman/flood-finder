A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

Before you head out.

Do I need an account to use the map?

No. You can browse reports and official hazards without signing in. An account is required to submit a report.

Are community reports verified?

New reports are unverified. An app reviewer can update their status, but that is not independent confirmation by an outside authority. Check timestamps, sources, and local official guidance.

Does an empty map mean it’s safe?

No. Coverage can be incomplete, reports can be outdated, and providers can be unavailable. The map is for awareness, not a guarantee of safe conditions or a flood-safe route.

Can I use this to request emergency help?

No. Flood Finder is not an emergency dispatch service. For immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Never enter or drive through floodwater.

ABOUT FLOOD FINDER

Local awareness starts
with shared understanding.

Flood Finder brings community observations and official weather-related hazard areas together, while keeping their sources distinct.

Built for everyday awareness

Flooding, blocked roads, power outages, storm debris, shelter needs, and other disruptions affect how people move through their communities. Our map helps people find context and contribute factual observations.

Official areas

Active National Weather Service warnings, watches, advisories, and relevant statements appear as shaded areas when usable boundaries are available. These areas can be broader than the actual hazard. Read the issuing agency’s instructions.

Community observations

Pins represent user submissions. Reports begin as unverified; review and response activity appear in their history. An app review does not imply government endorsement or outside verification.

Coordination with accountability

Residents submit reports. Administrators review them and manage response teams. Assigned team leads can acknowledge work and track progress. Creating an account does not grant response or administrator privileges.

PRIVACY & YOUR DATA

Know what you share.

Public reports are public information. Share observations about conditions, and keep personal details out of your submissions.

Account information

The service stores your name, email address, password hash, account role, and session records. Passwords are hashed, and sign-in uses an HTTP-only session cookie. Authorized administrators can access the user directory.

Public submissions

Report titles, descriptions, addresses, coordinates, timestamps, statuses, and operational histories are accessible to people using the service. Avoid names, contact details, medical information, and private locations you do not want to disclose.

External services

The browser requests map tiles from OpenStreetMap. That provider receives normal connection information such as your IP address. Server-side address searches use Photon, the U.S. Census geocoder, and Zippopotam.us; weather and hazard requests use National Weather Service services. Search text or coordinates are sent as needed, but report descriptions and account credentials are not included in those lookups.

Location and storage

Device location is optional and requires your permission. Signing out clears your session cookie. Account and report records remain on the service, and backups may retain records. This version does not offer self-service account deletion or password recovery.

SAFETY & RESPONSIBLE USE

Helpful context.
Careful decisions.

For immediate danger

Contact local emergency services. Submitting a report does not request or guarantee emergency assistance. Never drive through floodwater or enter a hazardous area to document it.

Contribute responsibly

Report what you observed, describe the location accurately, and avoid speculation. Do not submit harassment, private personal information, deliberately false reports, or content you do not have permission to share.

Check the source and time

Community reports may be inaccurate or outdated. Official alert boundaries are approximate, and provider outages or coverage gaps may hide relevant hazards. Follow local authorities and the instructions of the issuing agency.

Routes are estimates

Routing tools do not establish that a road is open or flood-safe. Follow closures and verified official navigation. Do not rely on Flood Finder as your only source of safety information.

USING FLOOD FINDER

Find a situation.
Check its source.

Search your area, read official boundaries, and contribute a factual report.

01 / SEARCH

Choose an area

Enter a five-digit ZIP (ZIP+4 also works) or a full U.S. street address and select Locate, then choose a radius. Or pan the map and select Search this map. The dashed circle marks your search area. Panning alone does not change the search.

02 / OFFICIAL AREAS

Read the affected region

Shaded regions come from National Weather Service warnings, watches, advisories, and statements. Click one for issuer text, timing, and instructions. A dashed boundary represents an affected zone, which may be broader than the actual hazard. A shape is not a claim that every street inside it is damaged.

When the provider has no usable boundary, a relevant notice stays in the list without an invented pin. No returned alert does not establish safety.

03 / COMMUNITY PINS

See reported conditions

Community pins describe flooding, access issues, outages, debris, shelter needs, and safety concerns people have submitted. New reports are unverified. Click a pin or list item to read the user submission, its original record link, and review history. No external citation is attached to community submissions. Safety guidance links offer general advice, not proof of that event.

This app does not automatically detect every emergency or turn a temperature/rain reading into a disaster report.

04 / FILTER

Choose the evidence you want

Switch between All sources, Official, and Community. The official-type filter applies to official hazards. Community type buttons can be combined; Community status, priority, sorting, and Only my reports apply to submissions. Text search can match either source. The reset arrow clears filters, while keeping your area and radius.

05 / REPORT

Use an address

Sign in and select Report a situation. Choose the type and severity, write a factual title and description, and enter a full street address. Publish resolves the address before submitting—no map click is required. If several addresses match, choose the right one.

The address goes to the U.S. Census geocoder; your account and report description do not. Report content and locations are public. Avoid personal names, phone numbers, and medical details.

06 / ADJUST A LOCATION

Use the optional map

For an addressless situation or a correction, expand the optional map section. Place/drag a marker, type coordinates, or use your device location with permission. Editing the address clears old coordinates. After submission, the search centers on your report.

07 / REVIEW

Coordinate a response

Administrators use Command center to review community incidents, manage roles, create teams, select leads, and dispatch work. Team leads use My assignments: Acknowledged, En route, On scene, then Completed. Rejected incidents cancel active work. Reopening with the same team creates a fresh assignment.

Official alerts are read-only source records. Community administration does not edit them.

08 / UPDATE STATUS

Understand the gaps

Reports update live and periodically. Official hazards refresh about every two minutes while visible. Partial coverage means some areas could not load; Unavailable means the feed failed or coverage is unsupported. Refresh can reuse a recent cached response.

NWS coverage is weather-related and point/state scoped; nearby cross-state alerts can be missed. It does not automatically provide every closure, outage, fire, or other local emergency.

Local awareness, not emergency dispatch

For immediate danger, contact local emergency services. Community reports, official areas, and estimated routes are not proof a road is safe. Never drive through floodwater.
